3. Mechanical Design Final Iteration

This section documents the final mechanical-iteration stage before full control implementation. The iteration focuses on gear-ratio redesign, custom bearing development, weight optimisation from jump-load constraints, and detailed mechanical refinements required for stable integration into the complete robot platform.

3.1 Wheel Inner Gear Ratio Analysis & Redesign
→

As the design progressed from a 3D-printed wheel prototype to the actual wheel design, the wheel size had to be increased because the initial 14-inch wheel was too small to accommodate the mechanical structure within the centre of the hubless wheel. It was therefore enlarged to 16 inches. Following this change, the gear specifications were recalculated and reselected to suit the updated wheel size while still maintaining sufficient mechanical advantage. In addition, to integrate the rubber tyre with the hubless wheel design, a custom rim was designed for CNC fabrication using carbon fibre plate, allowing the required groove profile to securely house the tyre.

As the motor specification sheet did not directly provide all required data points, a web-based digitisation workflow was used to extract the motor curve characteristics with higher resolution. The extraction was conducted using WebPlotDigitizer, and the recovered data was used as the numerical input for subsequent ratio-performance analysis.

The plotted effect of gear ratio on speed and acceleration (based on M3508 motor characteristics) indicates that the most efficient tradeoff region is around a gear ratio of 3.4. The final selected ratio was 3.76, as this was the closest practical involute gear ratio achievable from the gear-design workflow. The MATLAB script used to generate the graph and compute the data points is gear_ratio_speed_accel_analysis.m.

The pressure angle of the involute profile was evaluated against multiple transmission characteristics, as summarized in Figure 3.4. A pressure angle of 20° was selected as the final design value, as it provides a balanced compromise between load-carrying capability and meshing smoothness.

The gear-generator video records the profile-generation workflow and validates that the selected parameters produce a consistent tooth form ready for downstream CAD integration and fabrication detailing.

3.2 Custom Big Bearing Design

Due to weight and budget constraints, commercial large bearings were not adopted. A custom bearing mechanism was developed to allow independent rotation of the inner and outer wheel structures. The implemented concept uses two rows of bearings with eccentric adjustment to provide outward preload, while V-groove contact surfaces improve radial support and axial retention.

The design uses an inner V-groove bearing that is extended outward with an eccentric nut to apply tension preload. This preload pushes the bearing against the rim. The carbon-fiber rim is chamfered at the inner diameter so that the V-groove bearing can seat properly and constrain axial movement between the inner and outer wheel structures. The design progression from conceptual groove-profile selection to full-CAD packaging and final wheel assembly demonstrates that the custom bearing layout is mechanically feasible within the available wheel envelope.

The eccentric nut provides preload adjustability during assembly, allowing controlled bearing contact pressure and compensation for manufacturing tolerance. This mechanism improves rotational stability, reduces radial play, and supports smoother wheel motion under load.

The wheel rotates concentrically between the inner and outer wheel structures with minimal noise, and can achieve high rotational speed on the floor.

3.3 Weight Optimisation (From Jump Load)

A simplified energy-force calculation was used to define a conservative target load for structural validation:

Mass = 25 kg,   Jump height = 200 mm,   Mass per side = 12.5 kg

Potential energy per side: 12.5 x 9.81 x 0.2 = 24.525 J

Push-off distance = 50 mm,   Force = 24.525 / 0.05 = 490.5 N

With safety factor 3: design load = 1500 N per leg

In the following FEA figures, von Mises (N/m^2) is used as the equivalent stress indicator for yield-risk screening, URES (mm) is the resultant displacement magnitude for stiffness assessment, and ESTRN is the equivalent elastic strain (dimensionless) used to identify local strain concentration. 3.4 Other Mechanical Details

A 5 mm bolt with a lock nut is used to push down the tensioning block. The block then pushes the small tensioning wheel downward to tighten chain slack, without requiring motor re-spacing.

The chassis structure is arranged to provide a compact load path between wheel-leg modules and the main frame, while maintaining mounting interfaces for drivetrain components, sensing modules, and service access. A low-cost rectangle mounting block is also designed to hold the square extrusions together internally.

The custom tyre rim geometry is designed for the hubless wheel layout, providing a secure tyre seating profile and improved integration clearance with the surrounding drive and bearing architecture. The rim is also designed for low-cost manufacturing, as only 3-axis CNC machining is required to produce the common chamfer and groove features within the rim.
