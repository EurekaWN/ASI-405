1. Introduction

This project focuses on developing a bipedal wheel-leg robot for RoboMaster, with the aim of improving terrain adaptability while still meeting the speed, stability, and packaging requirements of competition use. The main motivation is to allow the robot to overcome obstacles such as the 200 mm step and handle more complex terrain more effectively than the team’s current chassis design.

At the first part of the project, the main focus was on validating the design concept and building the first working prototype. Go To First Part Website This included comparing different locomotion concepts, finalising the overall wheel-leg direction, and prototyping key mechanical subsystems such as the linkage, cascaded joint, hubless wheel, and chain drive. The goal of that phase was to confirm that the proposed design was mechanically feasible and could be developed further into a full robot platform.

At this part of the project (second part), the focus shifts from proof of concept to refinement and optimisation. Based on the prototype built in Semester 1, this phase focuses on improving the mechanical design, reducing unnecessary weight, increasing stiffness and reliability, and integrating the control system more fully into the robot. A significant portion of the work in this stage is dedicated to Dynamic Modelling, LQR balancing control, state estimation, actuation logic, and the overall firmware implementation needed to achieve stable and dynamic robot motion. The overall goal is to develop a more mechanically optimised and dynamically controllable robot that can move with stable balancing, terrain crossing, and full competition-level functionality.

However, the project was unable to fully reach its original goal, as budget limitations required the reuse of a malfunctioning motor, which greatly affected control accuracy and overall system performance. The project was still able to complete and validate the LQR capability of the overall system; however, full stable balancing tuning could not be achieved due to the same motor limitation.
