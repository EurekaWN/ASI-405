9. Project Value, Deliverables, and Moving Forward

9.1 Main Innovations

The project introduces a wheel-leg architecture that is specifically engineered to bridge the gap between high-speed wheel mobility and terrain adaptability. Its key novelty lies in the integrated mechanical-control co-design workflow: linkage kinematic modeling, force transmission mapping, reduced-order balancing control, and firmware-level safety implementation are developed as one coherent stack rather than as isolated subsystems.

The value proposition is practical and competition-driven. The platform targets obstacle-handling capability (including step-type terrain), while retaining compact packaging and dynamic balancing potential. This provides a stronger foundation than conventional rigid-wheel chassis designs for future RoboMaster-style terrain and mission requirements.

9.1.1 Main Mechanical Design Innovation

The four key mechanical innovations that define this design are summarized below.

Hubless Big Wheel Design

The hubless wheel architecture provides large effective wheel radius for obstacle crossing while keeping the center region open for drivetrain and structural packaging.

V-Groove Bearing Wheel

A V-groove rolling interface is used to constrain inner-outer wheel motion and improve concentric tracking under radial and axial load transfer.

Customised Bicycle Rim

The rim profile is customized for low-cost manufacturing and compatibility with the bearing path, balancing producibility with mechanical function.

Leg-Wheel Integration Structure

The final integrated CAD combines linkage, wheel module, and chassis interfaces into a compact wheel-leg assembly suitable for control and maintenance.

9.1.2 Competitive Advantage and Innovation

This solution is selected not only for the current competition constraints, but also to anticipate likely future rule and terrain evolution. The combined hubless big-wheel architecture, custom bearing-rim interface, and integrated wheel-leg structure provides a capability mix that is currently uncommon across global competition groups, giving the project a clear differentiation in both engineering approach and competitive utility.
